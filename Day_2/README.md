# Day 2 — DNA Extraction and Sanger Sequencing

## What I Learned

On Day 2, I practiced DNA extraction using an animal sample and learned more about DNA purification.

I also learned how Sanger sequencing works.

One of the most important concepts was the difference between dNTPs and ddNTPs. DNA polymerase normally adds dNTPs to extend a DNA strand. However, if a ddNTP is added, the DNA strand can no longer continue growing.

By producing DNA fragments that stop at different positions, scientists can determine the order of nucleotides in a DNA sequence.

## Experiment

I worked with an ant sample and extracted DNA from it.

I also used gel electrophoresis to examine DNA and learned about the principles behind Sanger sequencing.

## Methods

The ant sample was prepared and broken apart so that the cells could be lysed.

I then:

- added a lysis solution
- incubated the sample
- added silica to bind the DNA
- centrifuged the sample
- washed away unwanted material
- eluted the DNA into a new tube

The purified DNA could then be used for further analysis.

## Results

I successfully worked through the different stages required to separate DNA from the rest of a biological sample.

I also learned how gel electrophoresis can be used as part of the process of checking DNA before further analysis.

## What I Learned From the Results

The experiment helped me understand why DNA purification contains several different steps.

Binding allows the DNA to be separated from other material, washing removes contaminants, and elution releases the purified DNA so that it can be used in later experiments.

Learning about Sanger sequencing also helped me see how purified DNA can eventually be converted into sequence information.

## Reflection

I found Sanger sequencing especially interesting because a very small chemical difference between dNTPs and ddNTPs can be used to determine an entire DNA sequence.

It showed me how molecular biology and DNA analysis are connected.
