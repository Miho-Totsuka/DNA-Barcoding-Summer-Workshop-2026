# Day 4 — PCR Analysis and Nanopore Sequencing

## What I Learned

On Day 4, I learned how PCR products can be checked and prepared for DNA sequencing.

I also learned about Nanopore sequencing.

In Nanopore sequencing, DNA passes through an extremely small pore. As different nucleotides pass through the pore, they cause changes in electrical current.

A computer analyzes these changes and uses them to determine the DNA sequence.

I also learned how DNA sequence information can be used alongside physical characteristics, or morphology, when studying relationships between organisms.

## Experiment

I worked with the PCR products prepared previously and examined them using gel electrophoresis.

I also learned how DNA can be cleaned and prepared for Nanopore sequencing.

## Methods

I mixed PCR product with loading dye and loaded the samples into an agarose gel.

The DNA was separated using gel electrophoresis so that the PCR products could be examined.

The amplified DNA was then prepared for sequencing using purification steps.

I learned the basic Nanopore sequencing process:

`DNA → Nanopore → Change in electrical current → Base calling → DNA sequence`

## Results

Gel electrophoresis allowed the PCR products to be checked before sequencing.

The DNA could then be purified and prepared for sequence analysis.

## What I Learned From the Results

I learned that PCR and sequencing are connected parts of a larger workflow.

PCR produces many copies of the DNA region of interest, while sequencing determines the order of nucleotides in that DNA.

I also understood that sequencing machines do not simply "see" the letters A, T, C, and G. In Nanopore sequencing, the sequence has to be inferred from changes in an electrical signal.

## Reflection

Nanopore sequencing was especially interesting to me because it showed how biology, chemistry, physics, and computer science can all be involved in analyzing DNA.

I was surprised that DNA passing through a microscopic pore can create electrical signals that a computer can convert into sequence data.

This helped me understand more clearly how laboratory experiments eventually produce the digital DNA data used in bioinformatics.
