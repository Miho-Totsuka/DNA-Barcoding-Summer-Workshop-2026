# Day 3 — Soil DNA, PCR, and DNA Metabarcoding

## What I Learned

On Day 3, I learned how DNA can be studied from an environmental sample such as soil.

Unlike a sample taken from one organism, soil can contain DNA from many different organisms. This makes it possible to investigate a biological community using DNA.

I also learned about PCR and DNA metabarcoding.

PCR uses primers, DNA polymerase, dNTPs, and repeated temperature cycles to amplify a selected region of DNA.

DNA metabarcoding can then be used to study DNA from multiple organisms in the same environmental sample.

## Experiment

I extracted DNA from a soil sample and prepared DNA for PCR.

I also practiced calculating the concentrations and volumes needed for the experiment.

## Methods

I first processed the soil sample and separated the DNA from other material using centrifugation and purification steps.

I then prepared the DNA for PCR.

The PCR mixture included components such as:

- template DNA
- primers
- DNA polymerase
- dNTPs
- water

I also used the dilution equation:

`C₁V₁ = C₂V₂`

to calculate the amount of DNA or solution needed.

## Results

The DNA extracted from the soil was prepared for amplification by PCR.

The PCR products could then be checked and used for further DNA analysis.

## What I Learned From the Results

This experiment helped me understand why environmental DNA analysis is different from analyzing DNA from a single organism.

A soil sample can contain DNA from many organisms at the same time. PCR and metabarcoding provide a way to target and analyze that mixed DNA.

I also learned that the primers chosen for PCR are important because they determine which DNA sequences can be amplified.

## Reflection

DNA metabarcoding was one of the most interesting topics for me because it connects laboratory experiments with bioinformatics.

Instead of starting with one organism and asking what its DNA sequence is, metabarcoding can start with an environmental sample and ask what organisms might be represented by the DNA inside it.

This is closely related to my interest in environmental DNA research.
